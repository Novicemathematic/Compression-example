Missions

Open it in Logic Pro

  1. Select an audio track (a vocal, drums or bass works well).
  2. On the channel strip, click an empty Audio FX slot.
  3. Choose Dynamics ▸ Compressor.
  4. Play a loud section and watch the gain reduction meter.

Logic’s Compressor has several circuit types (for example Platinum Digital, Studio VCA, Studio FET and Vintage Opto). Each has its own character, and the exact controls vary by type.

A good workflow

  1. Set a moderate ratio such as 3:1 and a medium attack.
  2. Lower the threshold until the meter moves on the loudest parts.
  3. Adjust attack and release by ear.
  4. Add make-up gain to restore the lost level.
  5. Switch the plug-in off and on again. Match the loudness first, then decide if it really sounds better.

Simulator control ↔ Logic Pro control

ControlWhat it doesListen for
ThresholdThe level where compression beginsLower threshold means more of the signal is squeezed
RatioHow much the signal above the threshold is reduced. 4:1 means 4 dB over becomes 1 dB over.2:1 is gentle, 10:1 and above acts like a limiter
AttackTime taken to reach full gain reductionSlow attack lets drum hits poke through (punch). Fast attack tames them.
ReleaseTime taken to let go once the signal falls below the thresholdToo fast sounds jumpy. Too slow means the next note is squashed.
KneeHow smoothly compression starts around the thresholdSoft knee sounds more natural
Gain (make-up)Raises the whole signal after compressionRestores loudness, so the track can sit louder in the mix
